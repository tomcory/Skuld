Words used here

The analysis has three parts: O observes what the app does (its traffic, what it reads on the device), D reads what the provider declares (privacy policy, Data safety section, store listing), and E compares the two against data protection requirements. E first predicts what each practice is for (stage 1, reviewed on the other page), then raises concerns: a requirement that, by E's evidence, the app does not meet. A practice is one observed processing: data sent to a recipient, or read on the device.

Your verdict: does the concern hold? (H holds, D does not hold, U unsure)

You judge the app, not E. A concern says that the requirement (top box) is not met for its subject: the frame's question answered against the app. Holds: the requirement is not met, as the concern states. Does not hold: it is met, or the concern is wrong as stated. Unsure (left out of the rates): you cannot decide; say why in the note. E's status does not change what these mean: for a concern E did not establish, too, say whether it holds.

  • As stated means the requirement, the subject and the members. How E knows it (demonstrated, from documents) and how well it shows it are for the two criteria, not the verdict: a concern that is true but badly supported holds, with low criteria.
  • What you may use: the unit, the documents, general knowledge of the domain, and web searches about a recipient or service (its documentation, what it is). Say in the note what you looked up.
  • Several members: it holds if it holds for at least half of them; name the others in the note.
  • E's purpose is given. Where a concern is about a practice's purpose (an alternative, a purpose not specified), take the purpose as E states it: whether it is plausible is judged on the stage-1 page.
  • Conditional concerns ("if a DPO is designated"): take the condition as given; if you think it does not apply to this app, say so in the note.
  • Contradicted declarations: it holds if at least one listed practice is something the declaration, read with its own sentence, denies. Judge the provider's sentence, not the label D gave it. A denial of collecting or sharing is not contradicted by processing that stays on the device.
  • A less invasive alternative holds only if it would still serve the purpose as stated: an identifier that cannot do what the purpose needs is no alternative, however narrow.
  • Incomplete documents (e.g. a policy captured only as headings), where the concern rests on what they do not say: unsure.
  • If the requirement is not met for a different reason than the concern's, the concern does not hold as stated: note the other reason in the recall pass.
The levels of the criteria

One scale for both: yes, you would sign it as it is; mostly, after a small fix (one loose citation, one overstated word); partly, part of it is right and part is wrong or missing; no, it does not hold. For mostly and partly, pick one or more reasons (hover one for its meaning), or Other and say it in a few words: the level says how far, the reasons say why.

Evidence exists, cited correctly (1–5)
Yes: every item exists and says what E takes it to say. Mostly: one is cited a little loosely. Partly: some items do not say it, or one sentence is cut into fragments counted as separate evidence. No: the items the conclusion rests on do not exist or do not say it. None cited: E cites nothing, as for a concern resting on what the documents do not say; that is no fault.
E's conclusion follows (Q W E T)
Yes: it follows from the evidence. Mostly: slightly overstated, or a small gap. Partly: it follows for some members only, or the conclusion is right but a step to it is wrong. No: it does not follow.
Experiments count here: a success check that does not test the purpose (it only shows the app started, for a purpose of a later screen) or few runs on a side make the conclusion overreach. Evidence asks only whether the experiment exists and is what E says it is.
Declared purposes (phrases)

Mark each phrase specific, not specific or unsure (N: all not specific), first blind, then with E's output (your blind judgments are kept). The test: could a user tell from the phrase which processing it covers and why? Not specific: "to improve our services", "for marketing purposes", "for business purposes". Specific: "to send you a receipt for your purchase", "to show ads based on the apps you use". Read a phrase with its own sentence or table row and what that sentence directly introduces or refers to, not with explanations elsewhere in the policy; fragments of one sentence get the sentence's answer; a phrase that is no purpose (a heading, a feature name, a negation): unsure, with a note. Where E holds the phrases not specific, your verdict is derived from them (all not specific: holds); save with S.

Blind first and the recall pass
  • Some units are shown blind first (subject and question only): Requirement not met (F), met (M) or unsure (U), before E's output is shown; it cannot be changed afterwards. Not met is the same as a concern holding.
  • Recall pass (R), once an app's units are judged: a concern E should have raised and did not, which you would judge holds. One per line; not a narrower form of a concern E raised.
The evidence
  • Each item's edge shows its source: ● traffic, ● a screen, ● the provider's documents, ● E's corpus (documentation, norms), ● an experiment, ● a claim. A text several members cite is shown once, with how many cite it; repeated traffic findings are folded.
  • A screen may come with a screenshot and a model's description: for orientation, not evidence.
  • An experiment replays the app's launch or one of its features (as the exploration recorded it) with one thing changed: a recipient blocked, an identifier replaced, or the location made coarser. Its card shows the feature and the purpose tested, the change, and the success check (a screen to be reached, shown with its screenshot; a text to appear; or the first screen ready after launch). Each side ran several times: ✓ the check was met, ✗ it was not (hover for the run's traffic). Still worked means the check was met with the change as without it. The screenshots show where a run met the check, for orientation.
