What you judge. Each unit is one aggregated concern: concerns E raised that share a requirement, a step, a status and a key (a data type and purpose, a declared purpose, a declaration the processing contradicts, or a requirement on the documents). You see E's statement, its members, what it rests on and the provider's documents, and judge whether E is right as it states it. Which model produced the unit is not shown.

  • Evidence (1–4): the evidence exists and is cited correctly: yes, partly, no, or none cited. Each evidence item names its source (the practice as observed, traffic as captured, a screen of the app, the provider's documents, documentation or a legal norm from E's corpus, E's expectation stage); a text several members cite is shown once, with how many cite it. A screen may come with a description written by a model (purple box) and a screenshot (click to enlarge, Esc to close), as E's first stage was given them: the description helps you find your way, but it is a model's reading, not evidence. Yes: every item exists and says what E takes it to say. Partly: some items do not, or one sentence is cut into fragments that are counted as separate evidence. No: the items the conclusion rests on do not exist or do not say it.
  • Follows (Q W E): E's conclusion follows from that evidence: yes, partly, no. Partly: it follows for some members and not for others, or the conclusion is right but one step to it is wrong. A proposed alternative (data minimisation) counts only if it would still serve the purpose as stated: an identifier that cannot do what the purpose needs is no alternative, however narrow it is.
  • Phrases, for declared purposes: mark each phrase specific, not specific or unsure; N marks all not specific. Where E holds the phrases not specific, your verdict is derived from them (all as E says: agree; none: disagree; some: partly), and you save with Save (S). Read a phrase with the sentence or table row it stands in, and with what that sentence directly introduces or illustrates (the list after it, the example that follows) or explicitly refers to; not with explanations found elsewhere in the policy. Fragments of one sentence get the same answer as the sentence. A phrase that is not a purpose at all (a heading, a feature name, a negation, a definition): unsure, and say so in the note.
  • Your verdict on the concern as stated (every unit but declared purposes E holds not specific), after the criteria, which explain it: Agree (A), Disagree (D), Unsure (U, left out of the rates; say why in the note). A status "not established" is E saying it could not establish the concern: agree if that is the right call.
    • Several members: agree if the concern holds for at least half of them; name the others in the note.
    • Not established is the right call unless the unit itself shows what E needed (e.g. a cited passage that documents the alternative). If other units seem to decide the same question differently, say so in the note; do not let it change this verdict.
    • Conditional concerns (e.g. "if a DPO is designated", "if automated decisions are made"): take the condition as given and judge whether the documents lack what the concern says. If you think the condition does not apply to this app, say so in the note.
    • Contradicted declarations (a declaration in which the provider denies something, and practices observed that E holds contradict it): agree if at least one practice listed is something the declaration, read with its own sentence, denies. Judge the provider's sentence, not the label D gave it (its data type or recipient may be wrong): if the sentence denies nothing the practices do, disagree, and say so in the note. A denial of collecting or sharing is not contradicted by processing that stays on the device. Under a condition (e.g. "the recipient is not a service provider"), take it as given.
    • Incomplete documents: where a document is visibly incomplete (e.g. a policy captured only as its headings) and the concern rests on what it does not say, answer unsure.
  • Units of declared purposes show their phrases blind first: judge each phrase specific, not specific or unsure (N: all not specific) and save (S) before E's output is revealed; these blind judgments cannot be changed afterwards. With E's output shown, the phrases start from your blind judgments: change any that you now judge differently.
  • Some units are shown blind first: subject and question only. Decide Fails (F), Holds (H) or Unsure (U) before E's output is revealed; the blind decision cannot be changed afterwards.
  • Recall pass (R): once every unit of an application is judged, note what E should have raised for it and did not.
  • ← → previous and next unit, O next open unit, V show or hide the evidence, / search the document.
  • Values in code type (a data type, a purpose node) open their vocabulary when clicked: the tree with the value selected, its description, the broader node, the nodes alongside it and the narrower ones.
