Words used here

The analysis has three parts: O observes what the app does (its traffic, what it reads on the device), D reads what the provider declares (privacy policy, store listing), and E compares the two against data protection requirements. A practice is one observed processing: data sent to a recipient, or read on the device. E's stage 1, reviewed here, predicts what each practice is for (its purposes) and how far each purpose depends on the data and the recipient (its needs), without seeing anything the provider declares. Its later stage raises concerns, reviewed on the other page.

What you judge

Each unit is one practice (top box) and the purposes and needs stage 1 predicted for it. Judge them against what the stage saw: the sentence it was told (grey box) and, on the right, its traffic, screens and documentation passages. The stage never saw the provider's privacy policy or store listing: do not judge against them; if you know the provider says otherwise, put it in the note. Which model produced the unit is not shown. Work on your own.

What you may use: the context, general knowledge of the domain (what an ad network or an analytics service does), and web searches about a recipient or service (its documentation, what it is). Say in the note what you looked up. A claim E makes about this app still needs the context.

Rules for every question
  • One scale. Yes: you would sign it as it is. Mostly: after a small fix (one phrase, one loose citation, a neighbouring level). Partly: part of it is right and part is wrong or missing; it needs a rewrite. No: it does not hold. For mostly and partly, pick one or more reasons (hover one for its meaning), or Other and say it in a few words: the level says how far, the reasons say why.
  • One flaw, one question. An implausible purpose is a no on Plausible, not also on its node, need or steps. Judge the node, the needs and the steps for the purpose as E states it, even if you find it implausible.
  • Each purpose on its own wording. If two purposes overlap (the same purpose twice, or one a broader form of the other, e.g. Advertising and TargetedAdvertising), judge each as written, do not mark one down because of the other, and record the overlap under Overlaps.
  • E's status is not the question. Judge each purpose and need on the questions, whatever E's status: a not established purpose can still be plausible.
  • Unsure only where the context and a search do not let you decide; say why in the note.
The questions (the beside a question on the page opens its rule here)
Plausible purpose
Would someone who knows the recipient, seeing this flow, name this purpose? How much would you change E's wording before you sign it? Yes: nothing. Mostly: one phrase, e.g. personalised advertising where the flow shows only ad delivery. Partly: a rewrite, e.g. "analytics and fraud prevention" where only a fraud check is plausible, or it explains only part of the flow. No: it does not fit what the recipient does, e.g. app performance analytics for an ad network's ID sync.
DPV node
The node for the purpose as E words it. Exact: the most specific DPV node that covers the wording. Too broad: a more specific node covers it fully (Advertising for "ads chosen by your interests", where TargetedAdvertising fits). Too narrow: the node covers less than the wording. Wrong: another branch (name a better one if you can). A broad node is exact where no narrower node fits. Click the node to see it in the DPV tree, with the practice's other purposes marked (P2, P3 …).
Beneficiary
Whose interest the purpose serves, as worded; where two fit, the one the processing exists for:
  • user: a feature the user uses (syncing notes, a map, signing in, the weather where they are);
  • provider: the app provider's own interest: its analytics and crash reports, revenue from ads it shows, attribution of installs, fraud and security checks;
  • other: the recipient's or a third party's own interest: building its profiles, targeting ads across apps, data it passes on.
Right or wrong; ads in the app are the provider's unless the purpose as worded is the recipient's own profiling.
Role
Right or wrong. Functional only if the feature the user is using needs the processing; ads, consent dialogs, analytics, and resources loaded for an ad are ancillary, even when they appear on screen. Where the screens do not show which, decide by what the recipient does.
Cited parts show its outcome (purposes E holds supported)
Do the cited parts show the purpose's outcome happening in this app, including what sets it apart (for personalised ads, something personalised, not just ads)? Mostly: a minor gap. Partly: only the broader activity, or only some parts. No: none, e.g. only a general guideline.
Overlaps (optional)
Pick the other purpose, then same, broader (this one is a broader form of it) or narrower. A grey hint shows where the DPV nodes nest; judge the purposes, not only the nodes. Overlaps change no other answer.
Stated need plausible (per need)
Is the level E states (essential / quality dependent / not dependent) the one you would choose? "Without it" means the component removed and nothing in its place: the data not sent or read at all; for the recipient, the data not sent to it and no other party doing its job. Whether something less invasive could replace it is the concern stage's question, not this one. Mostly: defensible, but you lean to the neighbouring level. Partly: clearly one level off. No: two levels off (essential against not dependent).
Steps follow from what they cite (per need)
Regardless of the level. A step is supported if what it cites shows it (click a chip to read it), or if it is general technical knowledge you would accept without a source: "a WebView sends a User-Agent", "an analytics SDK sends its events in batches". A claim about this app or this recipient is not general knowledge and needs a citation, even if you could confirm it elsewhere: "the app uses the ID to restore purchases". Traffic supports a step about what is sent, not one that says the data is needed because it is sent. Yes: every step the conclusion rests on is supported. Mostly: a minor step is not, but the conclusion does not rest on it. Partly: a step it rests on is an uncited claim about this app or recipient, or contradicts what it cites, but the rest still carries the conclusion. No: without the unsupported steps nothing carries it. A need E holds not assessable is not asked.
Purposes E missed
A purpose you would answer yes or mostly on Plausible that E did not give: one per line, not a narrower form of a purpose E gave; tick functional if it is the feature's own.

Reading the context (right)
  • Cited for (opens first) shows what E cites for the purpose you are on: traffic, then screens, then documentation. The other tabs hold everything the stage was given: ● traffic, ● screens, ● passages (documentation and norms).
  • Traffic: per request its method and address, where the data was found (a header, the body, the URL) and the data highlighted. Findings that repeat with only numbers changed are folded: +N more like this.
  • Screens: the screen's texts, a screenshot (click to enlarge) and a model's description. The description helps you find your way; it is not evidence, and E may not cite it. Some screens have none (the model gave none, or gave an empty one). The stage was sent up to eight screenshots per episode; you see all that were taken, except of screens with a privacy or consent notice, which the stage never sees.
  • A grey block is one visit: the same screen, its content changed in place (text typed, a box ticked). It has one screenshot and one description; before the change marks a screen shown by its earlier state.
